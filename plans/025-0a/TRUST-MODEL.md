## 3. Trust zones

No zone is trusted merely because it shares a project, host, database, or release with another zone. Local verification is the default (Plan 025 sections 3 and 5).

| Zone | What it may assert | What it must prove | What it may never be trusted for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Writer | Receipt of bytes; proposed append position; proposed checkpoint | Canonical envelope acceptance, predecessor/position continuity, append result, and checkpoint consistency to the previous accepted cut (`INV-001` through `INV-007`, `INV-014`) | Its own authorization; correctness of a verifier verdict; completeness/non-equivocation without independent witnesses; caller-supplied time as authority |
| Verifier | A scoped claim or, exceptionally, a signed receipt containing the section 7 fields | Q1/Q2/Q3 separately against pinned trust root, policy, and cut; bounded parsing; deterministic results (`INV-019` through `INV-023`, `INV-045`) | Choosing trust roots/cuts from request data; turning signature validity into authorization; claiming global completeness outside the declared scope; correctness of a receipt when its operator is compromised |
| Witness | Observation and retention of a specific checkpoint; consistency with its prior observation | Witness identity/signature, monotonic retained state, checkpoint consistency, and quorum under pinned witness policy (`INV-015` through `INV-018`) | Event semantics, signer authorization, writer correctness, or independence when it shares the writer/DBA failure domain |
| Gate engine | A policy decision and context-bound decision event | Authenticated caller, required scoped claims, deterministic versioned policy, separation of duties, and atomic decision/action binding (`INV-024` through `INV-029`) | Raw DB rows, app flags, unsigned PASS values, transport identity, or a signature-only result as authority |
| Capability broker | Execution admission/denial and a broker audit event | Valid `CapabilityGrant`; caller/harness identity; executable digest/owner/safe resolution; safe environment; live short-lived credential (`INV-030` through `INV-034`) | A path name as executable identity; caller-controlled secret plane; provenance semantics beyond consuming scoped claims; a wake message as a grant |

## 4. Questions

The three questions are answered independently (Plan 025 section 4):

| Question | Answer | Answering zone | Pinned input |
|---|---|---|---|
| Q1 Authentication: did key K produce these exact bytes? | Verify a canonical, domain-separated signature that binds the full security context. Report algorithm, key id, byte/evidence digest, and result; do not infer authority (`INV-001` through `INV-004`, `INV-019`). | local verifier | protocol version/algorithm policy and trust-root digest from signed project policy |
| Q2 Authorization at position: was K authorized for this action there? | Establish inclusion at position `p`; replay the authenticated authority/policy state through `p-1`; evaluate the event action against that state; apply any later revocation-with-effect-range known at cut `c`; report authority result and policy version separately (`INV-008` through `INV-013`, `INV-020`). | local verifier | authenticated cut `c`, authority-policy version, trust-root digest, event position/proof |
| Q3 Completeness and non-equivocation: is the declared evidence scope complete, ordered, durable, and not forked or omitted? | Verify inclusion/consistency proofs, range boundaries/counts, checkpoint freshness, and independent witness quorum; compare retained prior checkpoints; state the exact completeness scope (`INV-005` through `INV-007`, `INV-014` through `INV-018`, `INV-021`). | local verifier plus independent witnesses | expected checkpoint/witness policy from signed project policy; verifier-retained prior cut; declared chain range/scope |

A claim is authoritative only for the questions and scope whose result fields are positive. No aggregate boolean may erase a negative, absent, or unknown sub-result (`INV-022`).

## 5. Temporal semantics

Let the authenticated log be an ordered sequence of positions `1..c`. A cut `c` is usable only when its checkpoint and required witness observations satisfy `INV-014` through `INV-018`. Let `S_p` be authority and policy state after deterministically applying accepted events through position `p`; `S_0` is the separately authenticated genesis state.

**Authority-at-position.** For an event proposed at position `p`, the verifier first establishes Q1 and inclusion/ordering. It evaluates the requested action using `S_(p-1)` and the event's bound action, subject, project, role, and policy context. If authorized, the event is accepted. If it is an authority transition, its deterministic transition function then produces `S_p`; otherwise `S_p = S_(p-1)`. An event cannot authorize itself. Genesis is the only special case and must be admitted by the signed bootstrap-root policy and bound first-write procedure (`INV-008`, `INV-009`). Position is established by the authenticated chain/checkpoint, never by an event timestamp, DB sequence supplied without proof, or request field (motivated by `SEC-04`, `SEC-07`, `cairn-03`; `INV-010`).

**Permission-now.** Permission for a new action is evaluated against `S_c`, where `c` is the newest accepted authenticated cut satisfying the freshness policy in section 9, plus the current signed deployment/profile policy. It is not inferred from historical authorization of an old event. A rotated, expired, superseded, or revoked credential has no permission-now unless `S_c` explicitly grants it for that action (`SEC-01`, `SEC-03`, `crypto-2`, `aw-4`, `aw-9`, `acb-12`; `INV-011`). Wall-clock time may enforce signed credential expiry and freshness only after the verifier has selected pinned state/cut; callers cannot supply the authoritative clock value.

**Verifier-knowledge-at-cut.** A verifier result at cut `c` is a function of the evidence, trust-root digest, authority-policy version, witness policy, and all authority events included through `c`. It must not claim knowledge of events after `c`. Re-evaluation at later cut `d > c` may change an authorization result if `d` contains a valid revocation-with-effect-range, policy correction, or fork evidence. Claims therefore bind `c`, the checkpoint digest, and verifier version (`INV-012`, `INV-019`, `INV-020`).

**Revocation-with-effect-range.** A revocation event at position `r` contains: revocation id; subject principal/key/delegation; affected action domains and projects; `effect_from_position = e`; optional `effect_through_position = t` (otherwise infinity); reason code; compromise-known-at position if distinct; issuer/approver identities; authority-policy version; and predecessor/context binding. The event itself must be authorized using `S_(r-1)` and required dual control. Once accepted at cut `c >= r`, Q2 for every otherwise-authentic subject action at position `p` where `e <= p <= t` returns revoked at knowledge cut `c`. The original signature remains a true Q1 observation; revocation changes Q2 and does not erase bytes or prior verifier knowledge. Ranges are log positions, not caller timestamps. A range cannot alter the validity of the revocation event itself or positions outside its declared scope (`INV-013`).

`OPEN:` The owner must approve which authority roles may issue retroactive effect ranges, the maximum permitted lookback, required dual-control roles, and whether `effect_through_position` may be finite. Until selected in signed policy, retroactive revocation issuance is denied by default (`INV-013`, `INV-028`).

## 9. Degraded state

`ASSUMPTION:` Signed project policy defines `AUTHORITY_CACHE_TTL`, `CHECKPOINT_MAX_AGE`, witness quorum, and trusted clock source. Proposed defaults are `AUTHORITY_CACHE_TTL = 5 minutes` with a hard maximum of 15 minutes, and `CHECKPOINT_MAX_AGE = 15 minutes` with a hard maximum of 60 minutes. `OPEN:` The owner must approve these values in 0C from the 0B operational evidence. A value above the hard maximum requires a new signed policy version and security review, not runtime configuration (`INV-010`, `INV-012`, `INV-016`, `INV-025`).

| Failure | Required behavior | Recovery |
|---|---|---|
| Authority resolver unavailable | Existing evidence may be displayed with its bound cut. A new authority-bearing write, gate action, or capability release may use a verified cache only when its key exactly matches evidence digest, trust-root digest, authority-policy version, and cut, and its age is within `AUTHORITY_CACHE_TTL`; otherwise deny. No stale cache extends permission-now (`INV-011`, `INV-012`, `INV-020`, `INV-022`). | Reconnect; fetch and authenticate a cut at least as new as the cached cut; replay authority transitions; invalidate affected cache entries; record degraded/recovery kernel events (`INV-049`). |

Recovery never chooses a caller-supplied cut, silently discards a fork, or rewrites an earlier verdict. Full replay and projection rebuild are required after rollback/restore (`INV-017`, `INV-038`, `INV-039`).

**Break-glass.** `ASSUMPTION:` Break-glass requires two authenticated people: one bootstrap-root operator (`ADV-25` role) and one distinct security/governance approver who is not the requester, signer of the affected action, DBA, verifier operator, or sole witness operator. It is invoked through an offline signed emergency-policy event containing incident id, reason, exact affected boundary/action, start cut, expiry no greater than 60 minutes, recovery target, and both identities (`INV-013`, `INV-028`, `INV-036`). `OPEN:` The owner must name the organizational roles and custody procedure.

Break-glass may select a pre-approved recovery endpoint, stop writes, isolate a component, restore pinned configuration, or permit narrowly scoped recovery diagnostics. It cannot make Q1/Q2/Q3 positive, accept unsigned/unverifiable legacy evidence, rewrite/delete kernel history, reduce witness quorum, issue a `CapabilityGrant`, release production secrets, satisfy independent review, or admit the business action whose evidence is unavailable (`INV-018`, `INV-020` through `INV-023`, `INV-028`, `INV-030`, `INV-048`, `INV-050`). Invocation, attempted prohibited use, every recovery action, expiry, and closure are context-bound kernel events. If the kernel cannot append, the independently signed emergency record is held by both operators and must be the first reconciled event after recovery; no ordinary operation resumes before reconciliation (`INV-049`).

## 10. Legacy evidence

`OPEN - OWNER DECISION REQUIRED BEFORE THE FIRST SLICE ADMITS LEGACY EVIDENCE:` Recommend **quarantine-as-unverifiable** for legacy v1-v5 evidence.

Reasoning: default acceptance preserves the exact downgrade, algorithm-confusion, retired-key, online/offline-divergence, and missing-context failures demonstrated by `crypto-1`, `crypto-2`, `SEC-03`, `cairn-10`, and `cairn-12` (`INV-001` through `INV-003`, `INV-007`, `INV-011`). A one-time re-anchor proves only that particular legacy bytes were possessed at re-anchor time; it cannot reconstruct original authority-at-position, completeness, non-equivocation, or trusted time. ADV-24 plus ADV-13/ADV-29 could forge authentic-looking legacy content and place it into rewritten history before re-anchor, while ADV-27 could endorse a matching fork. Permanent immediate rejection is cryptographically clean but discards potentially useful historical leads before migration classification and incident/legal-retention decisions.

Under the recommendation, legacy bytes are isolated from authoritative stores and projections, marked `legacy=true`, carry Q1/Q2/Q3 as unknown or negative as applicable, cannot satisfy a gate, claim, capability, review, authorship, timestamp, or completeness requirement, and may be viewed only as explicitly untrusted historical material (`INV-003`, `INV-020` through `INV-023`, `INV-040`, `INV-050`). A migration-boundary checkpoint records inventory digests without blessing content. After the dated compatibility window, quarantine becomes rejection as required by Plan 025 section 8 (`INV-050`).

## 11. Data boundary

This system attests agent development work on code and artifacts. It is not designed to ingest, retain, or attest patient data. The boundary is a layered risk reduction, not a cryptographic proof that PHI is absent (Plan 025 section 1; Sol re-review 3 blocking item 4).

1. **Allowed-content definition and minimization.** Accepted content is limited to canonical security events, small approved textual governance metadata, digests, proof material, and claim fields. Raw source trees, logs, model transcripts, screenshots, arbitrary files, secrets, patient records, and clinical content are outside the kernel content model. Artifact bodies are represented by digests unless a later signed policy narrowly adds an allowed class (`INV-044`, `INV-045`). `OPEN:` 0A-4 must finalize the exact allowed field/class schema and retention periods.
2. **Scanning and refusal as defense in depth.** Allowed textual inputs are scanned before retention; suspected PHI or secrets are refused and generate only a minimal content-free audit event. Scanning does not widen the allowlist and a clean scan is not evidence of absence (`INV-046`).
3. **Opaque/encrypted artifacts.** Opaque, encrypted, binary, compressed, or otherwise non-inspectable artifacts are digest-only by default. The kernel does not decrypt or retain their body; parser/decompression limits apply before processing (`INV-045`, `INV-047`).
4. **Quarantine, deletion, and incident handling.** Suspected captured PHI is access-restricted and handled through a named incident process covering containment, assessment, required notification/escalation, legally authorized deletion, backups/caches/downstream copies, and audited disposition. Accidental capture does not cancel HIPAA obligations (`INV-048`). `OPEN:` The owner must name the privacy/security incident owner, notification path, deletion authority, and retention/legal-hold policy before production data is permitted.
5. **Residual statement.** No signature, digest, checkpoint, scan result, or claim can prove that evidence contains no PHI. PHI can be hidden in allowed-looking text, encoded data, metadata, binaries, screenshots, logs, source, or transcripts, and classifiers have false negatives (`INV-044` through `INV-048`).

## 12. Residuals

The following remain unprotected or protected only under assumptions:

- A holder of a valid signer key can produce authentic signatures in that key's permitted domain. Attribution does not prove who physically acted, human intent, freedom from coercion, or absolute non-repudiation. Key theft before detection remains indistinguishable from authorized key use for Q1; later effect-range revocation changes Q2 only.
- A compromised local verifier, verifier host, or verifier operator can lie to consumers that trust its receipt/output. Local proof verification and differential testing reduce but do not eliminate compromise of the consumer's own verifier and pinned inputs.
- Writer/DBA plus the full required witness quorum can create or sustain a consistently witnessed fork. Independent witnesses reduce the collusion set; they do not make collusion impossible.
- Bootstrap-root operator/signing-quorum compromise can replace future trust roots, policy, deployment profiles, and binaries. It cannot make old independently retained checkpoints disappear, but clients that accept the malicious new root can be deceived going forward.
- A compromised gate engine or broker host can admit actions or expose credentials available in that boundary despite sound kernel evidence. Kernel audit may expose the act later; provenance is not OS enforcement.
- A compromised dossier/browser/session can mis-render the subject or trick a human. A valid review claim proves the recorded identities, subject digest, policy, and actions under the controls; it does not prove the human understood the change.
- Endpoint identity, project-admin authority, socket-peer identity, harness membership, environment safety, process exit semantics, secret-backend administration, and browser security are not supplied by the provenance kernel. They depend on their owning boundary's invariants and platform controls (Plan 025 section 5).
- External anchors prove observations under their custody and policy. Unless 0C selects an accepted TSA and validation policy, `TrustedTimestamp` cannot claim RFC 3161-equivalent trusted time; log order alone is not civil time.
- Availability is not guaranteed. Denial by a writer, verifier, witness quorum, authority resolver, gate, broker, host root, network, or resource-exhaustion adversary can stop protected actions because the design fails closed.
- Quarantine and deletion cannot undo prior PHI disclosure or guarantee removal from every backup, legal hold, external system, screenshot, or adversary copy. Absence of PHI cannot be inferred cryptographically.
- Opaque artifact digests authenticate equality only. They reveal neither safety, semantic content, authorship, license status, malware absence, nor PHI absence.
- Legacy v1-v5 evidence cannot recover missing original context, authority position, completeness, or trusted time through later anchoring. The section 10 recommendation remains open until owner decision.
- The claim names and auditor analogies are internal engineering hypotheses until the staged validation required by Plan 025 sections 7 and 9. Different model lineage alone is not organizational independence or ITGC segregation of duties.
- The 134 unreproduced Daybreak findings remain hypotheses at their stated severities until 0A-2. This model cites them as threat motivation, not as independently verified facts.
- Receipt policy, witness quorum/cadence, cache bounds, retroactive-revocation roles/lookback, break-glass roles, allowed-content schema, incident ownership, substrate, and process/repository placement remain open as marked. Claims must disclose applicable policy values rather than imply these choices are settled.
