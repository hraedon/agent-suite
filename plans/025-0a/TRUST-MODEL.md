## 3. Trust zones

No zone is trusted merely because it shares a project, host, database, directory, secret backend, or release. Local verification is the default.

| Zone | May assert | Must prove | Never trusted for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Writer | receipt/proposed append/checkpoint | canonical acceptance, continuity, signed acknowledgement, checkpoint consistency and rotation authority | own authorization; completeness/non-equivocation without witnesses; caller time |
| Verifier | scoped claim or policy-selected receipt | Q1/Q2/Q3 against independently acquired pins and one cut; deterministic bounded parsing | request-selected roots/cuts; signature-valid = authorized; choosing a fork branch |
| Witness | observation/retention of checkpoint | identity, monotonic state, consistency, observation time, operationally independent quorum | event semantics; signer authority; independence while sharing prohibited dependencies |
| Gate engine | policy decision and bound decision event | caller, claims, expected-state predicate, policy, SoD, atomic admission | raw rows, app flags, unsigned PASS, transport identity, stale state across lock |
| Capability broker | execution admission and audit | grant, platform-authenticated caller/harness, executable handle/image, safe environment, live credential | pathname identity; caller secret plane; wake as grant; kernel-compromised enforcement |
| Bootstrap root | pin/policy/profile transition and installation | independent pin acquisition, signed artifacts, key custody, monotonic floors, restore verification | runtime gate decisions; rewriting history; weakening policy by emergency rollback |
| Apps | commands, untrusted display metadata, human/agent input | re-authorization, projection replay, exact-subject binding, browser/session controls | holding governance-authority private keys; trusted claims; projection as authority |
| Transport | authenticated delivery metadata | source/destination/body/nonce/idempotency and bounded validity | transition, gate, capability, secret, or identity authority at receiver |

`ASSUMPTION:` An anchor is a witness function that externally observes and retains checkpoints, not a separate trust zone. Witness and anchor functions may be combined only if the selected design still satisfies the operational independence predicate in `INV-016`.

## 5. Temporal semantics

Let accepted log positions be `1..c`; `S_0` is the independently pinned genesis state. Verification is one deterministic ordered evaluation:

1. **Select inputs.** Acquire genesis/root/witness-roster digests under `INV-052`; select one authenticated cut `c`. A detected fork is terminal as specified below.
2. **Establish immutable acceptance once.** Starting from `S_0`, process positions in ascending order. At `p`, establish Q1, inclusion/order, schema/policy, and authorization against acceptance state `S_(p-1)`. If accepted, apply its transition to produce `S_p`; otherwise preserve `S_(p-1)`. Acceptance is immutable after this pass and an event never authorizes itself. Position, not caller time, controls authority.
3. **Collect operative revocations.** Every accepted revocation event is operative if its acceptance-time revocation authority and dual control were valid. Record the complete acceptance-time authority chain for each accepted revocation `R`. That chain is exempt from later Q2 changes only for the purpose of keeping `R` accepted and operative. Every event in the chain remains subject to step-4 marking and step-5 propagation for every other purpose, including its own cut-relative Q2 and authority it confers to any other event or principal. Later Q2 changes therefore never deactivate `R`, but they may make its recorded authority basis ineffective for all uses other than preserving `R` itself.
4. **Apply direct Q2 effects.** In revocation-event position order, mark each accepted event whose position/action/project falls in an operative effect range as `revoked-at-cut-c`. A mark on a revocation or its recorded acceptance-time authority chain does not deactivate that revocation under the purpose-limited step-3 exemption. For every other purpose the mark applies normally. Overlap is idempotent; no mark is removed.
5. **Propagate transitively.** In one forward position pass, authority conferred by a marked event is ineffective from that conferment onward for every purpose except preserving an accepted revocation that recorded that event in its acceptance-time chain. A delegation whose authority derivation includes ineffective authority is marked; every later action or delegation whose only derivation includes it is then marked. Direct actions in an operative effect range remain marked by step 4 whether or not they confer authority. Continue through position `c`. The pass terminates because marks only change once from effective to ineffective over a finite sequence; the purpose-limited preservation of an already accepted revocation adds no unmarking transition and no event is revisited to restore authority.
6. **Derive permission-now.** Evaluate new actions against the effective state at `c`, the policy/version floors, and the cut-observation age rules in section 9. Historical acceptance never supplies permission-now.
7. **Issue or deny.** Bind the result to cut/checkpoint/root/policy/verifier version and all Q1/Q2/Q3 sub-results. An unselected prerequisite, stale cut, unacknowledged required append, or terminal fork denies the positive claim/action.

**Chosen retroactivity rule and cost.** Retroactive Q2 revocation propagates transitively through authority conferred inside its effect range. This closes the SEC-01 class but can invalidate a large descendant set and requires deterministic replay at every later cut. The purpose-limited exemption in step 3 preserves each accepted revocation even when another revocation covers its grant, without preserving that grant's authority for any other use. An erroneous or malicious accepted revocation remains operative until superseded by the non-emergency ceremony in section 9; history is never rewritten. **OPEN (owner ratification):** D-0A-3 accepted the v0.3 exemption wording, so the narrowed exemption and its explicit irreversible-revocation consequence require owner ratification before section 5 is selected for deployment.

**Immunity-attack construction check.** Let root key `K_R` be compromised at position 9. The attacker appends delegations `D_1...D_n` from `K_R` to `M_1...M_n`; each `M_i` then issues an accepted throwaway revocation `R_i`, whose recorded acceptance-time chain includes `D_i` and the `K_R` binding. The genuine root later issues accepted `R*` with `effect_from_position = 9` against `K_R`. Step 4 marks the covered `K_R` events and every `D_i`. Step 5 makes every `M_i` delegation ineffective and propagates that loss to all other authority and actions derived from it. Step 3 nevertheless evaluates each `R_i` through its purpose-limited recorded chain, so every `R_i` stays accepted and operative. Marks remain monotone and the finite pass terminates.

**Mutual/circular revocation.** If two accepted revocations cover one another, both remain accepted, authorized, and operative by step 3; both effect sets are unioned and propagated. There is no oscillation and neither revocation cancels the other.

**Checkpoint key rotation.** A checkpoint committing through position `n` is authorized by effective writer-signing state after accepted event `n`. If a writer-key rotation occurs within the checkpoint range, the new key signs; the old key may also sign only during an explicit, position-bounded overlap encoded by the rotation event and policy. A checkpoint signed only by a key ineffective at `n` is rejected (`INV-014`).

**Fork terminal state.** Inconsistent checkpoints, pin/log equivocation, or two quorum-witnessed descendants from one predecessor produce `FORKED`, a terminal verification state for that root/policy epoch. The verifier selects neither branch, computes no authoritative `S_p`, returns Q2/Q3 unknown/negative, denies new claims/actions, and invalidates previously issued claims whose cut is at or after the divergence for future machine decisions. Earlier claims remain historical statements but must carry a fork warning when re-presented. Recovery requires a distinct signed non-emergency root/policy ceremony and permanent disclosure; it never rewrites the losing branch.

A revocation event binds revocation id, subject, domains/projects, `effect_from_position`, optional `effect_through_position`, reason, compromise-known-at position, issuers/approvers, policy version, and predecessor/context. Per D-0A-3 in `DECISIONS-0A.md`, signed policy requires bootstrap-root operator plus security approver dual control, allows unbounded lookback and finite `effect_through_position`, and denies retroactive revocation issuance until those values are selected in policy (`INV-013`).

## 9. Degraded state

`ASSUMPTION:` Signed policy sets `PERMISSION_NOW_MAX_STALENESS`, per-action stricter staleness maxima, the revocation-publication bound, `CHECKPOINT_MAX_AGE`, `WITNESS_CLOCK_SKEW_MAX`, `UNANCHORED_WINDOW_MAX`, `BREAK_GLASS_INCIDENT_MAX`, `BREAK_GLASS_CROSS_INCIDENT_MAX`, `WITNESS_RETENTION_MIN`, trusted observation clocks, and independent authenticated external time references. Each witness compares its observation clock with those references and the quorum's signed observations; unavailable comparison or skew above `WITNESS_CLOCK_SKEW_MAX` suppresses positive trusted time and Q3 rather than selecting a convenient clock. Correlated compromise of the selected references remains residual. Proposed `PERMISSION_NOW_MAX_STALENESS` is 5 minutes with a hard maximum of 15 minutes; proposed `UNANCHORED_WINDOW_MAX` is 60 minutes. `OPEN:` Owner approval of these values, `CHECKPOINT_MAX_AGE`, time references, `WITNESS_CLOCK_SKEW_MAX`, and the default `WITNESS_RETENTION_MIN` awaits 0B evidence. No runtime configuration or emergency policy may raise a hard maximum or lower a security minimum (`INV-055`).

Cache age is `decision_time - earliest_required_witness_observation_time(cut)`, never cache-entry creation or re-derivation time. Every cache entry binds the first authenticated observation time. Re-verifying or copying the same cut cannot extend the window. Each action policy sets a criticality floor no weaker than the global maximum; revocation, role grant, review acceptance, secret release, and release/root changes require a cut newer than the signed revocation-publication bound.

**Break-glass.** `ASSUMPTION:` Invocation needs a bootstrap-root operator and a distinct security/governance approver. Neither may be requester, affected-action signer, DBA, verifier operator, any required witness operator, directory/IdP admin, secret-backend admin, bootstrap-host admin (`ADV-19` capability), release/root signer for the affected policy, or operator of the affected boundary. The proposed signed defaults are `BREAK_GLASS_INCIDENT_MAX = 60 minutes` cumulative per incident and `BREAK_GLASS_CROSS_INCIDENT_MAX = 120 minutes` per rolling 24 hours for the same approver set and affected boundary across all incident ids. `OPEN:` The owner must approve those defaults and name roles, custody, approver rotation pool, and ceremony.

The cumulative authorization budget is bounded per incident, not per invocation, and also across incident ids by `BREAK_GLASS_CROSS_INCIDENT_MAX`; changing an incident id never resets the cross-incident counter. No renewal by the same set is permitted; any extension requires a different pre-authorized approver set and a non-emergency escalation ceremony, and cannot exceed either signed cap. Emergency records are pre-numbered and predecessor-chained under `INV-049`. Break-glass may stop/isolate, select pre-approved recovery endpoint, restore configuration only if `INV-055` floors and maxima do not weaken, or run narrow diagnostics. It cannot make Q1/Q2/Q3 positive, accept legacy, delete/rewrite history, reduce witness/retire/algorithm/version/custody floors, raise staleness/checkpoint/unanchored/break-glass maxima, issue grants, release production secrets, satisfy review, or admit the unavailable-evidence business action. `ADV-34` defeats the emergency approval assumption but cannot make violating records cryptographically conform without also defeating enforcing boundaries.

## 11. Data boundary

The system attests development work, not patient data. This boundary reduces risk; it cannot prove PHI absence.

1. **Allowed content/minimization.** Canonical structured security fields, digests, proofs, and claim fields only; raw trees/logs/transcripts/screenshots/files/secrets/patient records/clinical content excluded (`INV-044`, `INV-045`). `OPEN:` 0A-4 must finalize exact schema and retention.
2. **Scanning/refusal.** If signed policy admits textual retention, defense-in-depth scanning precedes it; refusal emits content-free audit and never widens the allowlist. Under the recommended no-free-text default, input is refused without claiming that the conditional retention control was exercised (`INV-046`).
3. **Opaque artifacts.** Opaque/encrypted/binary/compressed bodies are digest-only and bounded before processing (`INV-045`, `INV-047`).
4. **Incident handling.** If signed policy admits textual retention and suspected PHI is detected, it is restricted; process covers containment, assessment, notice/escalation, side-store/projection/cache/downstream handling, legal holds, and audited disposition. Under the no-free-text default, rejected input receives content-free refusal audit and no retained-text response is claimed (`INV-048`). `OPEN:` Owner must name incident owner, notice path, deletion authority, and retention/legal-hold policy.
5. **Immutability residual and schema choice.** Content admitted into a witnessed append-only event cannot be deleted without breaking the chain; deleting a projection does not delete the event or witness commitment. `OPEN - OWNER DECISION:` Recommend default **(a) forbid free text entirely in the allowed-content schema**, explicitly because Plan 025 makes ingesting, retaining, or attesting patient data a non-goal. Alternative **(b) redactable-by-construction** stores only a salted per-field digest in the event and plaintext in a separately access-controlled, deletable side store; salt custody/erasure and side-store references must not permit dictionary recovery. Choosing (b) consciously narrows that non-goal by admitting textual ingestion and creates the associated compliance and incident-response liability; it is not merely an implementation variant of (a). Until the owner selects (b), free text is forbidden and conditional `INV-046`/`INV-048` retention branches are inactive. No signature, digest, checkpoint, scan, or claim proves PHI absence.

## 12. Residuals

- A valid key holder, `ADV-24`, can sign within its permitted domains; Q1 does not prove human intent, coercion absence, or physical actor.
- Compromised verifier/output can lie to a consumer that trusts it; local verification reduces but does not eliminate compromise of the consumer platform.
- Writer/DBA plus full witness quorum can sustain a witnessed fork. A detected fork is terminal; availability and post-divergence claims are lost until a disclosed new-root ceremony.
- Root/signing compromise can replace future pins/policy/artifacts. Already-retained independent checkpoints expose conflict; a first-contact party with no independently authenticated pin can conclude internal consistency only and can be shown an entirely attacker-selected history.
- A writer can censor a submission that it never acknowledges. A signed acknowledgement makes later omission detectable, not preventable; acknowledgement withholding remains an availability/censorship residual.
- A compromised gate/broker/kernel can admit actions or expose credentials available there; provenance is not OS enforcement.
- Dossier/browser compromise can deceive humans. `ADV-10` resistance in section 7 applies only to independent machine consumers.
- Directory identity, harness membership, browser security, and secret administration depend on named platform controls. Shared UID, self-asserted adapter, or app session alone is insufficient.
- Permission-now can lag a published revocation by at most the applicable cut-observation/action floor. This bounded revocation-propagation latency is intentional; higher-criticality actions require a stricter floor.
- Trusted observation time depends on the signed external time-reference set and skew threshold. Cross-witness/reference comparison detects bounded skew, but correlated compromise or common-mode failure of those references can misstate time; positive trusted time and Q3 deny when comparison is unavailable or outside threshold.
- Witness fork detection reaches only as far back as retained signed state. `WITNESS_RETENTION_MIN` is policy-bound but its default remains owner-open; evidence older than available retained cuts cannot regain positive Q3 by assumption.
- An unanchored range remains permanently less trustworthy: later consistency proves append-only extension, not contemporaneous creation/order. `unanchored=true` can never be cleared from intersecting claims.
- `ADV-34` can abuse emergency authority within available enforcement; cumulative caps, rotation, and chained records make abuse bounded/auditable, not impossible.
- Availability is not guaranteed; fail-closed denial can stop protected actions.
- Admitted event content cannot be deleted from a witnessed append-only chain. Quarantine/deletion can address side stores, projections, caches, and controlled backups only; prior disclosure, external copies, witness commitments, and legal holds remain.
- Opaque digests prove equality only, not safety, meaning, authorship, license, malware absence, or PHI absence.
- Without selected TSA/time policy, `TrustedTimestamp` must not issue. Log order is not civil time.
- Legacy cannot recover missing context, authority position, completeness, or trusted time by re-anchoring. Section 10 remains owner-open.
- Claim names/auditor analogies remain internal hypotheses. Model lineage difference alone is not organizational independence.
- The 134 unreproduced findings remain hypotheses at Daybreak severity until 0A-2; citations motivate threats, not verification.
- Receipt policy, witness cadence/quorum above two, staleness values, checkpoint age, time references/skew, witness-retention duration, retroactive roles/lookback, break-glass caps/roles, content schema, incident ownership, substrate, and placement remain open as marked.
